# Topic 38 — Feedback: How Prophet ﷺ Gave Constructive Feedback to Companions

## Introduction

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was not only the Messenger of Allah but also an excellent teacher, leader, mentor, and guide. Throughout his life, he taught his Companions how to live according to Islam and how to improve themselves. Since the Companions were human beings, they sometimes made mistakes or misunderstood certain matters. The Prophet ﷺ corrected these mistakes, but his method of correction was characterized by wisdom, patience, kindness, and understanding.

Constructive feedback means giving advice or correction in a way that helps a person recognize a mistake and improve without unnecessarily humiliating or discouraging them. The Prophet ﷺ demonstrated this quality repeatedly. He did not make people feel worthless because of their mistakes. Instead, he used their mistakes as opportunities for education and personal growth.

His ﷺ method of giving feedback provides an important model for students, teachers, parents, friends, and leaders. It teaches us that correcting someone is not simply about pointing out what they did wrong; it is about helping them become better.

---

## 1. Correcting Mistakes with Kindness

One of the most important characteristics of the Prophet's ﷺ method was kindness. Even when someone made a serious mistake, he did not immediately humiliate or insult that person.

A famous example is the incident of a Bedouin who urinated inside the mosque. The Companions were understandably disturbed and wanted to stop him. However, the Prophet ﷺ told them to leave him alone until he had finished. Afterwards, the Prophet ﷺ ordered that water be poured over the affected area. He then explained the proper purpose of the mosque.

This incident is narrated by Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه in Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith No. 219. In the Bukhari narration (220), the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "You have been sent to make things easy and not to make them difficult."

The Prophet ﷺ understood that stopping the man abruptly could create a greater problem. Instead, he allowed the immediate situation to finish and then corrected the misunderstanding.

### Lesson

This teaches us that when someone makes a mistake, we should remain calm and think about the best way to correct them. Anger and embarrassment do not always produce improvement. Sometimes patience creates a much better opportunity for teaching.

---

## 2. Teaching Rather Than Simply Criticizing

The Prophet ﷺ did not limit his feedback to saying that something was wrong. He explained the correct behavior.

In the incident of the Bedouin, the Prophet ﷺ explained that mosques are places for the remembrance of Allah, prayer, and recitation of the Qur'an, rather than places for impurity.

Therefore, the man did not merely learn that he had done something wrong. He learned why it was wrong and what the correct behavior should be.

This is an important feature of constructive feedback. Simply saying:

> "You did this wrong."

does not necessarily teach a person how to improve.

A better approach is to explain:

1. What was wrong.
2. Why it was wrong.
3. What should be done instead.

The Prophet ﷺ used mistakes as teaching opportunities.

---

## 3. Avoiding Harsh and Insulting Speech

The Prophet ﷺ was known for excellent manners and did not use vulgar or insulting speech.

Abdullah ibn Amr رضي الله عنه reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was neither obscene nor one who intentionally used obscene language. He also emphasized the importance of good character.

This narration is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith No. 6029.

> "The best among you are the best in character and manners."


This is particularly important when giving criticism. A person may need to hear that their action was incorrect, but that does not mean they need to be insulted.

For example, there is a major difference between saying:

> "This behavior was wrong."

and saying:

> "You are a terrible person."

The first addresses the behavior, while the second attacks the person's character.

The Prophet's ﷺ example teaches us to correct actions without unnecessarily destroying a person's dignity.

---

## 4. Considering the Person's Situation

Another important feature of the Prophet's ﷺ teaching was that he considered the circumstances of the person receiving the advice.

People have different levels of knowledge, different personalities, and different experiences. Therefore, the same advice may not be suitable for everyone in exactly the same way.

The Prophet ﷺ taught his Companions with wisdom and chose appropriate methods depending on the circumstances.

He also advised his Companions not to make religious teaching unnecessarily difficult. In Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith No. 69, the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Facilitate things to people, and do not make it hard for them, and give them good tidings and do not make them run away."

This principle can be applied to feedback as well. Good feedback should help a person improve rather than make them feel that improvement is impossible.

---

## 5. Choosing the Appropriate Time

The Prophet ﷺ also demonstrated wisdom in choosing appropriate times for teaching and advising people.

Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith No. 68, records that the Prophet ﷺ would take people's circumstances into consideration when giving them advice and knowledge so that they would not become bored or overwhelmed. The narration states:

> "The Prophet used to take care of us in preaching by selecting a suitable time, so that we might not get bored."

This shows that when advice is given can be just as important as what is said.

For example, if a student makes a mistake in front of the entire class, immediately embarrassing them may make them defensive or ashamed. A teacher may achieve a better result by calmly explaining the mistake and, when appropriate, discussing it privately.

The Prophet's ﷺ example therefore teaches us to think before giving criticism:

* Is this the right time?
* Is this the right place?
* Does this person need correction immediately?
* Can I explain it without embarrassing them?

---

## 6. Encouraging Good Character

The Prophet ﷺ did not only correct negative behavior. He also praised and encouraged positive qualities.

In Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith No. 6035, the Prophet ﷺ emphasized that the people most beloved to him were those with the best character and manners:

> "The most beloved to me amongst you is the one who has the best character and manners."

This demonstrates another important principle of feedback: people need encouragement as well as correction.

If someone only hears criticism, they may begin to believe that they are incapable of doing anything correctly. On the other hand, recognizing good qualities can motivate a person to continue improving.

Constructive feedback can therefore contain both:

Recognition of what is good + Correction of what needs improvement.

This creates a balanced approach.

---

## 7. Giving People an Opportunity to Improve

A mistake does not have to define a person.
(People aren't bad it is their habits which are bad and if they fix them they would be good people.)

The Prophet ﷺ understood that people could learn from their mistakes and change their behavior. His approach was therefore focused on improvement rather than simply punishing someone for what had already happened.

The incident of the Bedouin demonstrates this clearly, as recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. After the mistake, the Prophet ﷺ did not turn the man into an object of ridicule. Instead, the situation became a lesson about the respect and cleanliness required in a mosque.

This teaches us that feedback should have a purpose.

The purpose should be:

Mistake → Understanding → Correction → Improvement

rather than:

Mistake → Humiliation → Discouragement

---

## 8. Respecting People's Dignity

Respect was an important part of the Prophet's ﷺ interactions with people.

Even when correction was necessary, he did not make a person's mistake an excuse for unnecessary humiliation. This is especially important because people are more likely to accept advice when they feel respected.

A person who is publicly insulted may focus more on their embarrassment than on the actual lesson.

On the other hand, respectful advice allows them to think about their behavior and make a genuine effort to improve.

Therefore, constructive feedback should protect a person's dignity while still making the necessary correction clear.

The Prophet's ﷺ forbearance is also demonstrated in Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith No. 6088:

> "While I was going along with Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) who was wearing a Najrani Burd (sheet) with a thick border, a bedouin overtook the Prophet (ﷺ) and pulled his Rida' (sheet) forcibly. I looked at the side of the shoulder of the Prophet (ﷺ) and noticed that the edge of the Rida' had left a mark on it because of the violence of his pull. The bedouin said, "O Muhammad! Order for me some of Allah's property which you have." The Prophet (ﷺ) turned towards him, (smiled) and ordered that he be given something."

## 9. Practical Principles of the Prophet's ﷺ Feedback

From these examples, several principles can be identified.

### 1. Be Kind

Correction should be given with compassion rather than unnecessary anger.

### 2. Focus on the Action

Criticize the behavior that needs changing rather than attacking the person's entire character.

### 3. Explain the Reason

People are more likely to learn when they understand why something is wrong.

### 4. Give a Solution

Do not only identify the problem. Explain or demonstrate the better alternative.

### 5. Consider the Situation

Think about the person's knowledge, circumstances, and emotional state.

### 6. Choose the Right Time

Sensitive criticism should not automatically be given publicly.

### 7. Encourage Improvement

Make the person understand that mistakes can be corrected.

### 8. Avoid Insults

Harsh or humiliating language can make feedback less effective.

### 9. Be Patient

Some people need time and repeated guidance before they change.

### 10. Have a Good Intention

The purpose of feedback should be to help the person improve, not to make ourselves feel superior.

## 10. Application in Our Daily Lives

The Prophet's ﷺ approach to constructive feedback is highly relevant to our lives today.

### At School

Students make mistakes in assignments, presentations, and group work. Instead of making fun of someone who makes a mistake, classmates should help them understand what went wrong.

Teachers can also use constructive feedback by explaining mistakes clearly while encouraging students to improve.

(X teacher in our college thinks that if students got bad marks it is their fault, but the fault is mostly 2 sided in reality. They (teacher) was being harsh because students didn't get good marks and as a result the students feared the teacher and the cycle of fail -> learn -> do better ended.
)

### With Friends

Friends sometimes make mistakes or behave in ways that may hurt others. Instead of immediately insulting them or discussing their mistake with everyone else, we should advise them respectfully.

### In the Family

Parents and children can also benefit from this approach. Correcting a child with patience and explanation can teach them much more effectively than simply shouting at them.

### In Leadership

A good leader does not make people afraid of making mistakes. Instead, they create an environment where people can learn from mistakes while still being held responsible for their actions.

---

## Conclusion

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ gave constructive feedback through a combination of wisdom, kindness, patience, respect, encouragement, and clear guidance. When his Companions or others made mistakes, he did not humiliate them or destroy their dignity. Instead, he corrected them with gentleness, explained the reasoning behind the correction, and gave them the opportunity to improve. His ﷺ method remains a timeless model for anyone who wishes to give feedback that truly helps others grow.
